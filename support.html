Alarm One User Support Guide

Thank you for choosing Alarm One. This guide helps you get the most out of your alarm experience and troubleshoot common issues.

Contact support

Before you create an alarm

  • Use a device supported by the current App Store listing. Alarm One requires iOS 26 or later.
  • Allow Alarm One’s alarm access when prompted. If access is off, review the app’s permissions in Settings.
  • Save a short test alarm while awake and check its sound, date, and next occurrence.
  • City-based alarms require Pro. Importing your own audio file requires Pro and iOS 26.1 or later.

Find the setup for your schedule

  • Work/rest rotation and 4-on-4-off setup
  • An alarm on a specific future date
  • An alarm in another city’s time zone
  • An alarm that deletes itself after use
  • Import a supported audio file

If an alarm did not ring

  1. Confirm the alarm is saved and enabled in Alarm One. Clock alarms and Sleep/Wake Up schedules are separate.
  2. Review alarm access, the selected sound, AM/PM, the next date/time, and any skipped occurrence. For Rotation, check the first work date and work/rest counts; for a city alarm, check the city and local-time conversion.
  3. On iOS 27: If the phone lit up or the flashlight flashed with no sound, check Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Alarms and Timers, update a paired Apple Watch to watchOS 27 (or consider unpairing an unsupported watch), and run a locked two-minute Clock test. Silent mode and Focus do not mute Clock alarms.
  4. Third-party apps (including Alarm One): Alarm One alarms can also be affected on iOS 27, and we are sorry for the hassle. Until Apple ships a fix, please complete the volume and Watch checks above, run a locked test, and keep a Clock backup for any alarm you cannot miss. For background, see TechBetweenTheLines on Feedback FB23754550.
  5. Create a short test while you are awake. If it still fails, email support with your iOS version, Alarm One version, schedule settings, and what you expected versus what happened.

If the dates do not match your roster

Rotation uses consecutive work days followed by consecutive rest days with one alarm time. Skipping an occurrence leaves that cycle in place. Change the start date only when your cycle changes. For irregular shifts or different start times, use separate dated alarms.
