iPhone Alarm No Sound on iOS 27: When the Screen Lights Up and Flashlight Blinks

The alarm goes off on time. The iPhone screen wakes up. The rear flashlight may blink. And then, nothing happens. There is no sound, and often no vibration either. Some owners hear the tone about half a minute late, while others sleep right through it.

Users have reported this exact pattern on iOS 27 since mid-September 2026. An Apple Support Community thread started around 16 September 2026 gathered about 106 “me too” replies by 29 October 2026, with owners still describing the issue on iOS 27.2. Apple had not issued a public statement as of early November 2026. TechBetweenTheLines and Chasms cover the same morning experience: the alarm UI starts, but the audio fails.

Here is a Clock-first troubleshooting checklist, followed by notes for third-party alarm apps. It does not claim any single app is immune.

What you are probably seeing

In typical reports, the alarm time and toggle look right. When the alarm triggers, the display lights up (and the flashlight may flash), yet there is no ringtone and often no haptic feedback. Most users have already ruled out Silent mode and Focus, and Apple states that those modes do not mute Clock alarms. Some owners note that a quick test works while the phone is unlocked, only to fail when the iPhone is locked on the nightstand (as noted in community reports covered by TechBetweenTheLines).

Rule out ordinary culprits first: Sound set to None (which only vibrates), StandBy mode (which disables alarm haptics), and headphones or a Bluetooth speaker left connected. If your iPhone lights and flashes with no audio, walk through the iOS 27 checks below.

1. Raise the new Alarms and Timers volume

iOS 27 split system volumes. Under Settings → Sounds & Haptics, alarms and timers now have their own volume slider instead of always matching your ringtone. After updating, some people find Alarms and Timers sitting low even when their ringtone volume feels fine.

  • Open Settings → Sounds & Haptics.
  • Find Alarms and Timers.
  • Either drag that slider up, or turn Match Ringtone Volume on so alarms follow your ringtone level again.

Next, set a short test alarm. If low volume was the only problem, you should hear it right away.

2. Update (or carefully unpair) Apple Watch

The clearest fix in the community thread, as reported by TechBetweenTheLines and Chasms, involves a paired Apple Watch.

  1. On your iPhone, open the Watch app → My Watch → General → Software Update.
  2. Install watchOS 27 if your watch supports it.
  3. Test the alarm again with the iPhone locked.

Apple’s compatibility list for watchOS 27 includes Apple Watch SE 3, Series 9 and later, and Ultra 2 and later. Older watches that cannot install watchOS 27 leave you with a mismatched pair. At least one owner reported that unpairing that older watch restored alarm sound and vibration on the iPhone. Unpairing backs the watch up to your iPhone, though you will need to set the watch up again. Only take this step if you accept that trade-off.

Sleep schedule note: If you wear your watch to bed with a Sleep schedule, the iPhone may stay quiet on purpose while the watch taps your wrist. To make the phone ring as well, look for Always Play on iPhone under the sleep alarm’s Alarm Options (confirm the exact labels on your iOS 27 build).

3. Confirm the alarm has a real sound

In Clock → Alarms, edit the alarm → Sound. If Sound is set to None, your iPhone only vibrates. Pick a built-in tone instead. Soft surfaces make a vibrate-only alarm easy to miss, especially in StandBy mode where Apple disables alarm haptics.

4. Run a locked two-minute test

  1. In Clock, create an alarm for two minutes from now.
  2. Lock the iPhone and place it face-up on a hard surface (not under a pillow).
  3. Walk away and wait.
  4. Pass = you hear the tone and feel a vibration (unless you intentionally use a silent haptic setup).

Passing this test after the volume and Watch checks means you are in good shape for tomorrow. If the test still fails after those steps, keep a separate backup device tonight and contact Apple Support with your iOS version and whether an Apple Watch is paired.

5. Extra checks when the alarm is only “too quiet”

These steps are less specific to the flash-with-no-sound bug, but they help when the tone simply feels soft (see also iPhoneWired’s Face ID list): turn off Attention Aware Features under Settings → Face ID & Passcode (and Accessibility → Face ID & Attention) for a few nights; disconnect any headphones or speakers before overnight tests; and restart the iPhone before repeating the locked test.

6. If you use a third-party alarm app

Third-party apps rely on Apple’s alarm frameworks. They do not behave identically to every Clock edge case, and they can run into extra iOS 27 issues.

TechBetweenTheLines reported that in early September 2026, the developer of an AlarmKit-based alarm app filed Apple Feedback FB23754550. On iOS 27, the app was not notified that an alarm had fired until the user opened the app again (sometimes hours later), and the developer also saw late system alarm UI alongside a silent timer alert. That Feedback remained open when they published their report.

Both TechBetweenTheLines and Chasms give the same bottom line: for anything you cannot miss, set a backup alarm in Clock until Apple ships a fix.

Fix checklist

  1. Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Alarms and Timers: raise the slider or turn Match Ringtone Volume on.
  2. Clock → Alarms → edit → Sound: choose a built-in tone, not None.
  3. If you have Apple Watch: Watch app → My Watch → General → Software Update → install watchOS 27 when available.
  4. Sleep schedule + watch in bed: enable Always Play on iPhone if you want the phone to ring too.
  5. Watch too old for watchOS 27 and nothing else works: consider unpairing (you will need to set the watch up again).
  6. Optional: turn off Attention Aware Features for a test if alarms only feel soft.
  7. Restart iPhone; set a two-minute Clock alarm; lock the phone and confirm sound + vibration.
  8. Using any third-party alarm app (including Alarm One): add a Clock backup for must-not-miss times.
  9. Still silent with volume up, a real tone, and no outdated watch: use another device tonight and contact Apple Support; mention iOS 27.x and the flash-without-sound symptom.

FAQ

Why does my iPhone alarm light up or flash but make no sound on iOS 27?

Owners describe on-time alarms where the screen wakes and the flashlight blinks, but there is no sound or vibration (or the audio plays late). Reports started around 16 September 2026 and continued onto iOS 27.2. Check your Alarms and Timers volume first, then update any paired Apple Watch to watchOS 27 (or unpair an older watch that cannot update).

Do Silent mode or Focus mute my Clock alarm?

Apple states that Silent mode and Focus do not mute Clock alarms. Toggling those modes will not fix this symptom.

What should third-party alarm app users do?

Third-party apps, including Alarm One, can also be affected on iOS 27, and we are sorry for the inconvenience. TechBetweenTheLines reported developer Feedback FB23754550 regarding AlarmKit delivery delays on iOS 27. Until Apple ships a fix, complete the volume and Apple Watch checks, run a locked test alarm, and set a backup alarm in Clock for anything you cannot miss.
